Previous research on the classic Irish television series Derry Girls has focused largely on language and identity, social and political themes, or qualitative questions such as whether its speech authentically reflects Irish accents. The main contribution of this study is methodological. Focusing on a set of grammatical features characteristic of Irish English, we quantitatively compare a corpus of the show's scripts with ICE-Ireland to assess whether the series exaggerates these features, using normalized frequencies, observed/expected (O/E) ratios and chi-square tests. Furthermore, we use the same corpus to explore whether TF-IDF and Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) can effectively extract keywords and topics for individual episodes, and whether large language models such as GPT-4 can perform emotion detection on the dialogue.
